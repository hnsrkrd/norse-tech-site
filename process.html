For Miners
Responsible Gold Recovery

01 · CHARACTERISE THE ORE

The ore comes first.

The mine provides a representative ore sample to Norse Mining Center, NMC. Before any process or equipment is proposed, NMC first establishes what kind of material we are dealing with.

We look at the ore type, how the gold occurs in the material, whether it appears free or locked in other minerals, and whether physical recovery is likely to be suitable. Where required, this first assessment is supported by further laboratory analysis and test work.

The sample must represent the material that will actually be processed. A poor sample can lead to a poor process decision.

NORSE PRINCIPLEThe ore determines the process — not the other way around.

02 · TEST LIBERATION & RECOVERY

Find out what the ore actually needs.

Once the ore has been characterised, NMC tests how the gold can best be liberated from the surrounding material and then physically recovered.

Different degrees of crushing and grinding, particle-size ranges and physical separation methods may be compared. The aim is not simply to grind as fine as possible, but to find the point where the gold is sufficiently liberated without unnecessary processing or over-grinding.

This work also shows whether gravity-based recovery is suitable. If the gold remains locked in minerals that the NORSE physical process cannot treat effectively, that should be identified here — before equipment is selected.

NORSE PRINCIPLETest the ore first. Select the technology second.

03 · DESIGN THE PROCESS

Build more than one possible route.

Using the characterisation and test results, NORSE develops two or three alternative process-line configurations that appear technically suitable for the material.

The alternatives may use different combinations or sequences of crushing, grinding, classification and physical separation. The objective is not to decide immediately which design is best, but to create a small number of realistic process routes that can be tested against the same ore.

Each configuration is therefore built around what the ore has already shown us.

NORSE PRINCIPLEDo not assume the first design is the right design.

04 · RUN THE PROCESS

Test the alternatives as complete flows.

The selected process configurations are then run using the ore. Each setup is tested separately so that NORSE can see how the material behaves through the complete process, not only in an individual machine or laboratory test.

The alternatives can then be compared for recovery, process stability, material handling, operating complexity and overall efficiency.

A process that looks promising on paper can behave differently once the stages are connected. This step produces the measured results needed for the final decision.

NORSE PRINCIPLETest the complete process — not just the individual machine.

05 · SELECT THE PROCESS

Let the results decide.

When the two or three process configurations have been tested, their results are compared and NORSE selects the route that provides the most suitable overall solution for that specific ore.

The highest recovery is not automatically the best process if the setup is unnecessarily complex, unstable or inefficient to operate. The objective is a strong, repeatable and practical recovery process.

The selected configuration becomes the technical basis for the process line NORSE recommends for the project.

NORSE PRINCIPLEThe final process is selected by evidence.

Understand the ore. Recover physically where possible. Measure the result. Build trust into the process.
